## Two minute Q and A

Answer only the question asked. Then show one supporting appendix.

### Did this warning cause a company delay

“No. It is a real saved warning, but route exposure and delay are explicitly fictional assumptions. A warning starts a review. It does not prove a flood, road closure or MR D.I.Y. disruption.”

### What is the concrete better decision

“We compare responses under the same declared demand, arrivals and shared resources. In this replay, a transfer-plus-expedite package removes modeled unmet demand, while its 1.3-hour continuity gap remains visible. This is conditional decision evidence, not measured company benefit.”

### Does replanning improve everything

“No. Under the assumed scenario weights, replanning has known cost MYR161.016947 versus MYR273.972951 for a fixed two-decision plan. But weighted unmet demand is slightly higher: 2.240850 versus 2.155276 units. Both retain a 1.3-hour continuity gap. We show cost and service together.”

### Is this a trained agent or high fidelity simulator

“No. It is a bounded seven-day rehearsal with decisions on days 0 and 2. It enumerates declared actions and preserves resource balances. It does not implement RL, MCTS, PINNs or a calibrated physical simulator.”

### Does it know the future

“The next action sees only fulfilled synthetic sales, receipts, current balances and an assumed carrier ETA. Future demand and scenario identity stay hidden. Identical observed histories receive the same action.”

### What do the forecasting results prove

“A separate exploratory UCI study has weekly WAPE 69.73% versus same-fold mean28 77.21%. Daily error remains 108.53%, and the data had prior exposure. It is not company validation or a new untouched holdout.”

### Can it execute or approve the plan

“No. Event, joint and sequential comparisons are read-only. The optional GPT-OSS explanation and saved human choices remain selected-case-only. No order, payment, staff assignment or facility control executes.”

### What needs validation next

Observed company transactions, stock, arrivals and warning outcomes. No company partnership or pilot has been agreed. A real operating comparison must measure service, false alarms, useful lead time and decision costs under equivalent constraints.

## Evidence and numerical boundaries

### Three calculations that must stay separate

Static four-day replay: no-change unmet demand is 68 units. The inspected transfer-plus-expedite package has zero modeled unmet, known conditional cost MYR243.9675 and a 1.3-hour gap. Its own before/after cost is MYR259.9675 to MYR243.9675, a conditional holding-cost effect, not observed savings.

Sequential four-day replay: assumed-weight known cost is MYR161.016947 with replanning versus MYR273.972951 for the fixed comparator. Weighted unmet is 2.240850 versus 2.155276. Both gaps are 1.3 hours. Weights 0.5/0.3/0.2 are assumptions, not measured probabilities. The delayed branch plans both transfer and expedite.

Sequential two-day baseline: costs are MYR71.016947 versus MYR93.972951, and the delayed branch adds transfer only. Do not substitute these figures into the four-day story. The old constant-demand 40-unit gap and MYR496/61.6 comparison is separate again.

### External demand studies

The new UCI analysis uses eight disjoint 28-day test windows starting February–September 2011, each preceded by 28 validation days, with 14 fixed candidates. It has 89,324 weekly forecasts and 625,268 daily prediction points. Selector weekly WAPE is 69.7338%, mean28 77.2117%, and selector daily WAPE 108.5263%. The descriptive paired 400-resample weekly difference interval is −11.63 to −3.71 percentage points. One retailer, eight time blocks and prior exposure limit confirmation.

Original November–December 2011 daily test: mean28 MAE 6.974 and WAPE 103.41%; weekly-naive MAE 7.727; ridge MAE 7.607. Ridge lost. Different targets and periods prevent a direct before/after accuracy claim. Neither study validates company shortage probabilities or causal savings.
