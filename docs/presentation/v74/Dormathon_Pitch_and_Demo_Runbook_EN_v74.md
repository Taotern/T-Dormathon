“We compare packages against the same demand, arrivals and shared resource snapshot. This example moves modeled unmet demand from 25.636 to zero while showing the remaining 1.3-hour continuity gap. It proves a reproducible conditional comparison. Real benefit needs observed operational outcomes.”

### Is this actual MR D I Y data

“Only the public company context is real. The 196-day history and all internal operating inputs are synthetic. We imply no company access, endorsement or measured savings.”

### What is the predictive result

“In an exploratory eight-fold study of UCI retail sales, the past-validation selector has weekly WAPE 69.73%, versus 77.21% for mean28 on the same folds. Daily WAPE remains 108.53%. These periods had been exposed in earlier training analysis, so this is not a pristine holdout or company validation.”

### What forecasting is implemented

“Statistical methods with past-only validation selection. We compare 14 fixed point candidates, including seasonal, smoothing and intermittent-demand methods. An earlier supervised ridge model lost to the simple mean, so we report that result rather than promote it.”

### Is this reinforcement learning or a global optimum

“No. We enumerate 72 declared packages across six linked cases. Forty-eight are feasible under the default constraints. The frontier is relative to that bounded catalog and displayed objectives, with no hidden scalar weights.”

### Can the AI act or approve this plan

“The joint comparison is read-only. The optional GPT-OSS 120B explanation remains selected-case-only and does not receive the joint plan. Existing human-choice records also remain case-scoped. Nothing places an order, pays an invoice or controls equipment.”

### Are the forecast ranges calibrated

“No. The live low/high values are authored-history scenarios. They are not confidence bounds or stockout probabilities. Missing required inputs make the calculation abstain.”

## Study and source notes

### New rolling study

UCI Online Retail II is an external UK non-store retailer dataset from 2009–2011. The new analysis evaluates positive recorded sales, a demand proxy, in eight disjoint 28-day test windows beginning February through September 2011. Each fold uses its prior 28 validation days to select from 14 fixed statistical candidates. There are 89,324 weekly forecasts and 625,268 daily prediction points.

Exact aggregate weekly WAPE is 69.7338% for the selector and 77.2117% for mean28: a 7.4779 percentage-point difference. The descriptive paired 400-resample SKU/fold interval for selector minus mean28 is −11.63 to −3.71 points. Only eight time blocks, one retailer and prior exposure limit confirmation. Daily selector WAPE is 108.5263%. WAPE is an error ratio, not one minus accuracy.

The data belonged to prior training periods. Freezing the new protocol prevents fresh test-driven choices within this analysis but does not undo earlier exposure. Exact isolated reproduction and formula checks passed. No operational model promotion, calibrated stockout probability or causal savings follows.

### Original study remains separate

The original November–December 2011 daily test retains mean28 MAE 6.974 and WAPE 103.41%. Weekly-naive MAE is 7.727 and ridge MAE 7.607. Ridge lost to the mean. The new weekly score cannot be compared directly with this original daily score as a single improvement claim.

### Scenario costs and shared constraints

The v73 no-change known conditional cost is MYR323.583. The displayed frontier example is MYR63.968. These exclude unpriced energy, service and review consequences and are not realized savings. No-change continuity gap is 4 hours; the frontier retains 1.3 hours. Energy exposure alone does not establish a receiving delay. Invoice review is counted once across SC17/SC18.
